# Ówàńbè Design System — Aso-Oke Ledger

## 1. Concept & Metaphor

Ówàńbè turns celebration plans into comparable vendor offers, visible commitments and coordinated work. Its interface must help a host understand what is proposed, what is agreed and what has actually been paid.

**Aso-Oke Ledger** combines the ordered bands of woven aso-oke with the family contribution ledger: information is grouped into readable strips, with clear ownership and accountable totals. Apply this metaphor structurally, not as a decorative textile background.

- Arrange quotes and payment records in bands: identity, scope, money, deadline, then action. Keep related amounts and explanations together.
- Use Fraunces for event and editorial headings, Source Serif 4 for instructions and conversation, and IBM Plex Mono for transaction references and aligned financial figures. Never style every dashboard label as a display heading.
- Use soft containers to distinguish records without turning the workspace into floating tiles. Elevation indicates an actionable or overlaid surface, not importance alone.
- Motion confirms a recorded change or transition. Never animate a payment as completed before server confirmation.
- Cultural guidance must name its source and acknowledge family or regional variation. Do not present the weave as a universal symbol for every Nigerian celebration.

Reject white-and-gold luxury wedding templates, script fonts, soft gradients, purple or indigo gradients, three equal feature cards, emoji icons, floating glass cards, generic dashboard mockups and glossy three-dimensional blobs. These obscure the product’s practical responsibility: making commitments legible.

## 2. Section Diversity & Layout

Never build a page as hero → logo strip → three feature cards → pricing. Choose each section’s structure from the decision it supports.

- **Public explanation:** use an editorial narrative showing the journey from event intent to approved quote. Follow it with an annotated, authentic product screen or clearly labelled illustrative record—not invented traction.
- **Host first-run:** use conversational intent capture, then an editable summary of event date, location, guests and budget. Do not lead with an empty analytics dashboard.
- **Host workspace:** place the next required decision first, followed by deadline-oriented step lists and relevant vendor results. In thin-coverage locations, show the coverage limitation and an action to change location or service criteria.
- **Vendor discovery:** use portfolio-led results; use a comparison matrix once the host selects providers. Align inclusions, exclusions, availability and quote freshness rather than comparing headline prices alone.
- **Vendor house:** start with enquiries and quotes requiring attention. Reveal business-management depth on demand within the same vendor profile.
- **Financial and staff views:** use dense tables for reconciliation, verification queues and audit records. Open evidence beside the selected record instead of repeating summary cards.
- **Disputes and recovery:** use a dated timeline with evidence and the next responsible person. Put emergency replacement actions before historical detail.
- **Media pages:** use an attributed gallery with captions and consent-aware media, not the operational ledger layout.

Increase density for repeated records; reduce it around payment review, consent and irreversible decisions. On narrow screens, retain record labels and comparisons rather than dropping essential columns silently.

## 3. Copywriting & Action Precision

Headlines name an audience and an outcome. Never use “Welcome,” “Powerful tools,” “Elevate your workflow,” “Seamless experience” or “Unlock your potential.”

Use these headline formulas:

1. **[Audience] + [planning outcome]**  
   “Hosts, turn your celebration budget into a vendor shortlist.”
2. **[Audience] + [commercial action] + [clear result]**  
   “Vendors, send quotes with inclusions and expiry dates hosts can compare.”
3. **[Audience] + [urgent problem] + [recovery outcome]**  
   “Event hosts, find replacement providers when a vendor does not arrive.”

CTAs name the action and its result. Never use “Get Started,” “Learn More” or “Submit.”

1. **[Verb] + [object] + [destination]**  
   “Send quote to host.”
2. **[Verb] + [decision] + [next stage]**  
   “Accept quote and review payment.”
3. **[Verb] + [evidence] + [case outcome]**  
   “Send evidence for dispute review.”

A profile action that opens chat must say “Request booking quote,” not imply a confirmed reservation. Quote acceptance, payment initiation and confirmed booking require distinct labels.

Show currency, applicable timezone, fees, exclusions and cancellation consequences where a user decides. Do not describe an estimate as a quote or an initiated reversal as money already returned.

## 4. State Completeness

Every route and independently updating region must specify all six states in implementation acceptance tests. “Handled by the framework” is not a specification.

- **Empty:** state what is missing and give the exact next action. An empty shortlist offers “Find vendors for this event.” A host-only Vendor house entry explains the separate workspace and offers “Create vendor profile.” Empty recommendations must not produce invented providers.
- **Loading:** use structure-matched skeletons for initial vendor or ledger retrieval. Use labelled progress for quote generation, uploads, payment reconciliation and case creation. Show measurable progress only when available; otherwise name the current operation. Keep previously loaded records visible during refresh.
- **Error:** identify the failed operation, offer a safe recovery and state what survives. Preserve quote drafts, counter-offer text and recoverable upload references. For uncertain payment outcomes, offer “Check payment status,” not an immediate payment retry that could duplicate a charge.
- **Success:** confirm the actual persisted result and show the next step. “Quote accepted” leads to payment review; only confirmed payment can produce booking confirmation. Refund initiation and refund settlement remain separate.
- **Validation:** validate completed fields on blur and all fields on submit. Identify the field and correction, such as an expiry preceding the quote’s issue time. Preserve input and link the error summary to each invalid field. Do not interrupt typing with premature errors.
- **Offline:** label cached plans, quotes and availability as potentially stale. Allow supported local draft editing; identify queued changes individually. Do not queue payments, approvals, quote acceptance or payout changes for automatic execution. Authorized entrance scanners may queue check-ins against their cached guest list, but cannot reissue invitations.

On reconnection, fetch authoritative state before enabling commitments. Resend permitted queued operations with deduplication, show sync results and expose conflicts rather than silently overwriting newer records. Display outage-paused deadlines as paused, not expired.

## 5. Brand Personality

- **Accountable:** hosts and vendors need to distinguish promises from settled commitments. Every financial status must have a reference, timestamp and responsible next actor.
- **Considerate:** older relatives, diaspora organisers and busy providers may share planning responsibilities across distances. Explain unfamiliar terms beside the decision without assuming technical fluency.
- **Culturally attentive:** celebrations differ between families and communities. Ask about preferences; do not infer required rituals, spending or status from a person’s name or event type.

These qualities must appear in record structure and permissions, not ornamental claims about luxury.

## 6. Voice and Microcopy

Use warm, respectful guidance without impersonating an elder or treating adults as children. Be direct about money, deadlines and uncertainty. Avoid slang in financial notices and celebration jokes in disputes.

Use **Ówàńbè** in brand copy; accept **Owambe** through the normalized search alias. Validate cultural spelling and usage with qualified practitioners before launch.

Keep “You’re chatting with AI” visible in OwanbePlanner and OwanbeAI, alongside a response-reporting action. AI recommendations are proposals, never approvals or guarantees.

Example strings:

- **Empty:** “No vendors saved for this event yet. Find providers for your location and add them to your shortlist.”
- **Loading:** “Checking this payment with our payment partner. We have not confirmed your booking yet.”
- **Error:** “We could not send your counter-offer. Your message is saved here. Try sending it again.”
- **Success:** “Your revised quote has been sent. View the quote to check its expiry and the host’s response.”

Use these only when the underlying behavior is true.

## 7. Signature Motif

The alternating orange/teal selvage-stripe rule marks information boundaries: quote metadata, ledger sections, active workspace tabs and labelled workflow stages.

Use it once per meaningful boundary, not around every field. On payment and dispute timelines, pair each segment with a written stage; the stripe must not imply elapsed time or completion by itself.

Do not place it behind body text, inside evidence images, across QR codes, or over identity documents. Do not use it as confetti, a looping loader or a substitute for warning text. Verification accents require documented verification evidence and a readable explanation of what was checked.

## 8. Component Rules

Apply the generated component specifications without recreating their token inventory here.

- **Card:** use for a vendor profile summary, quote or portfolio item. **Do:** separate scope and price metadata with the ledger boundary. **Don’t:** nest cards beyond one level or turn every paragraph into a card.
- **Table:** use for payment records, verification queues and dispute lists. **Do:** group related status rows with labelled bands and aligned amounts. **Don’t:** introduce additional stripe colors or depend on tint to identify status.
- **Tabs:** use for peer workspace sections and team menus. **Do:** retain unread counts on their labels. **Don’t:** switch houses automatically when a cross-house notification is opened.

- **Alert:** use for payment outages, caution acknowledgments and AI disclosure. **Do:** keep consequential notices visible until their condition resolves or acknowledgment is recorded. **Don’t:** auto-dismiss compliance warnings.

## 9. Accessibility

Use the generated accessibility thresholds for text contrast and interaction targets; do not reduce them in dense staff tables or mobile quote controls.

- Keyboard-test the full path from vendor search through quote review. Every control needs visible focus; the selvage accent must supplement, not weaken, the focus indicator.
- Measure hit areas, including icon actions, tab labels and scanner controls. Adjacent payment and cancellation actions must not have overlapping targets.
- Check contrast in every status, including muted badges, disabled explanations, errors and striped table rows. No financial state may rely on color alone.
- Under reduced motion, remove sliding, scaling and stripe-fill animation while retaining immediate state updates.
- Use semantic headings, table headers, labelled controls and ordered timelines. Announce payment and sync changes without announcing every countdown tick.
- Trap focus in modal dialogs and return it to the initiating control. Link validation errors programmatically.
- Test zoom, narrow-screen reflow and complete diacritic rendering. Read amounts with currency and deadlines with timezone; never force screen readers to reconstruct a quote from visual columns.

## 10. The Skeptical Auditor Test

A generated screen passes only when all five checks pass:

1. **Five-second clarity:** a new visitor identifies celebration planning and vendor coordination, its host/vendor audience, and the next action. For authenticated screens, the current house and event are apparent.
2. **Evidence authenticity:** no fake logos, testimonials, counts, scores or commercial metrics appear. Demonstration content is explicitly marked as owner-replaceable placeholder content. “Verified” has inspectable evidence.
3. **Mobile usability:** at the required narrow mobile test viewport, the primary action remains reachable and legible, targets meet generated requirements, and nothing essential depends on hover. Financial consequences remain visible before commitment.
4. **Originality:** every banned generic pattern is absent. The banded ledger hierarchy and selvage boundaries reveal the concept without reading the copy.
5. **State and content coverage:** all six states have testable behavior. Each section supports a specific celebration-commerce decision rather than a template with substituted nouns.

## 11. Do and Don’t List for AI Coding Agents

**Do**

- Bind displayed statuses and available actions to authoritative workflow states and server-enforced permissions.
- Keep Host and Vendor data, unread activity and navigation context separate.
- Preserve estimates, quote revisions, accepted terms and settled payments as distinct records.
- Keep AI read-only for money investigations; require authorized humans for fund actions.
- Mask sensitive fields by role, including support views that cannot reveal payment details.
- Keep guest contact details hidden and allergy data restricted to its authorized purpose.
- Treat unresolved approval thresholds, retention conflicts and verification policies as release blockers for affected actions—not opportunities to invent rules.

**Don’t**

- Replace the ledger with a generic analytics dashboard, luxury wedding template or decorative card grid.
- Generate unearned trust badges, fabricated availability or guaranteed recovery promises.
- Enable money movement through optimistic UI, offline replay or AI-generated approval.
- Treat a disabled button as sufficient explanation for a locked cancellation or payout change.
- Expose restricted data in client payloads merely because its component is hidden.
- Claim an integration, registry search, cultural review or compliance agreement has been completed without evidence.

## UI Craft Rules for AGENTS.md

- Use the semantic tokens in this file for every colour, type, spacing, radius and shadow value; never introduce new ones.
- Never hardcode hex, rgb() or hsl() values, and never use arbitrary Tailwind values such as bg-[#...], text-[13px] or p-[7px].
- Every view must implement empty, loading and error states before it is considered done; no blank screens and no silent failures.
- Use the component inventory as the building blocks; a new component needs an explicit owner decision.
- Refuse the generic three-equal-card layout; choose the section pattern from the diversity rules and vary rhythm by content.
- No emoji icons, purple/indigo gradients or stock SaaS slogans; use the signature motif and voice defined here.
- Honour the motion ceiling (calm) and prefers-reduced-motion; motion confirms actions, it never decorates.
